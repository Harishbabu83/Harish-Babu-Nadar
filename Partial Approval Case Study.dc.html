LOS · 03 · Partial approval Loan origination system · Document verification

Keep the good files, ask only for the rest.

Applicants often send several files for one document — three months of salary slips, a multi-page bank statement. Partial approval lets the document verification team approve the files that pass, reject the rest with a clear reason, and send the applicant one precise instruction instead of a full re-upload.

01Problem & opportunity

One bad page, every file again.

Verifiers could only approve or reject a document as a whole. If one of three salary slips was blurry, the whole set was rejected, and the applicant was asked to upload everything again — often without knowing which file had failed or why. Good files were thrown away, and the same document came back for review twice or three times.

Opportunity

Let the verifier decide per file, explain a rejection in a consistent way, and carry that explanation straight to the applicant — so the next upload is the right one.

03
Applicants left guessingThe app said “rejected” but not which file, so applicants re-sent everything or the wrong thing.
02Approach

Precise for the verifier, plain for the applicant.

Four principles shaped the change without slowing down the common case — a document that simply passes.

Principle 01 Decide per file Every file in a document gets its own outcome, so one bad page never costs the good ones.
Principle 02 Reasons from a list Fixed categories make rejections consistent; a required instruction keeps them human.
Principle 03 Say the consequence “Only selected files will be approved, others will be rejected” — written into every confirm step.
Principle 04 Close the loop The verifier’s instruction appears in the applicant’s app, right above the Upload button.
Key design decisions
01A selection mode, not permanent checkboxes“Select files” turns the file tabs into checkboxes and becomes “Cancel selection”, so everyday review stays uncluttered.
02Actions follow the selectionWith files selected, Partial Approve switches on and Reject switches off — the buttons only ever offer what makes sense.
03Colour the tabs by outcomeApproved files turn green and rejected files red, so the result reads at a glance without opening each one.
04Pin the reason to the documentThe reject reason and instructions stay under the preview for anyone who opens the document later.
03Key flow & solution

From review to the right re-upload.

The document stays on the left with its files as tabs; extracted details sit on the right; the decision bar sits under the preview.

05Learnings

A rejection is a message to someone else.

01Granularity is a quality featureDeciding per file kept good uploads instead of throwing them away with the bad one.
02A reason list is also a datasetFixed categories show which documents fail most and why — something free text never could.
03Write for the person re-uploadingThe instruction is read by a student on a phone, so it’s required and shown exactly as written.
