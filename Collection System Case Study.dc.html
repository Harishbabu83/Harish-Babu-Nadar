03 · Collection System Collections 2.0 · Early-warning & recovery

The right nudge, at the right time.

Every overdue borrower used to get the same treatment: reminders, then calls, then a field visit. Collections 2.0 sorts accounts into seven risk segments, gives each a treatment pathway, and hands the team a ranked queue that says who to contact, when, how — and what it costs to recover.

Executive summary
From one escalation ladder to seven pathways.

Rules sort accounts every 15 minutes; each segment starts with the cheapest intervention likely to work. Humans are saved for the accounts that need them, and every action is measured against one number — the cost to recover ₹100.

Problem & opportunity

Same process, very different borrowers.

A student whose auto-debit failed on a bank error and a borrower drifting toward default were treated alike — the same reminders, the same call scripts, in the same order. Agents spent their day on accounts that would have paid with a WhatsApp message, while the risky ones waited. Nobody could say what each recovered rupee cost.

Opportunity

Read why an account is overdue, match it to the cheapest intervention likely to work, and escalate only when that fails — with one metric that rewards getting it right.

Approach

Seven segments, seven pathways.

Rules run every 15 minutes, top to bottom; an account joins the first segment it matches. Each segment begins with its own first move.

{{ s.name }} {{ s.when }} {{ s.type }}{{ s.action }}
Principle 01 Diagnose before acting Segments describe why someone is late, not just how late.
Principle 02 Cheapest effective first Digital where it works, people where it matters, field and legal last.
Principle 03 One answer per account The queue says who, when, how and why — ranked by risk and due time.
Principle 04 Rules ops can own Conditions, action, frequency and attempt caps — editable and switchable without a release.
Key design decisions
01Cost to recover ₹100 as the north starCure rate alone rewards over-calling; pairing recovery with cost makes the cheap, effective nudge the winning move.
02Next best action with its oddsEach borrower shows ranked options with a success probability and the best window to try — the agent picks, the system recommends.
03Start from the queue, in bulkFilter by segment or Digital/Human, select rows and Start all — routine nudges never need a borrower page.
04A live board for interventionsRunning outreach sits in status columns; dragging a card updates it, and live calls pulse so supervisors see what’s happening now.

Learnings

Empathy scales when it’s targeted.

01The metric shapes the behaviourOnce cost sat next to recovery, the team stopped treating every account as a call.
02Name the reason, not the risk“Salary delay” tells an agent what to say; “risk 45” doesn’t. Segment names became the script.
03Recommend, then step asideShowing odds and leaving the choice to the agent earned more trust than auto-assigning actions.
