LOS · 04 · CAM redesign Loan origination system · Credit appraisal

A credit memo that shows its working.

The CAM — credit appraisal memo — is where a credit manager decides whether to recommend a loan for sanction. The old tab was a long stack of tables with three unexplained scores. The redesign leads with one weighted score, breaks it into the parts that produced it, and puts each part’s score beside its data all the way down the page.

01Problem & opportunity

All the data, none of the reasoning.

The old CAM listed every parameter it had — course, institute, bureau, income, collateral — as rows of name and value. Scores lived in their own tab as three totals, with no way to see what pushed them up or down. Co-applicants sat behind separate tabs, and deviations were a flat list of values against criteria. Credit managers rebuilt the reasoning in their heads, case after case.

Opportunity

Make the CAM read top-down like a memo: the verdict first, the drivers next, and the evidence for each driver right where its score appears.

Three gaps
01
Scores without reasonsEligibility, PD and Kuhoo scores were totals only — nothing showed which inputs moved them.
02
One applicant at a timeBorrower and up to four co-applicants sat behind tabs, so comparing them meant clicking back and forth.
03
Deviations without weightSixteen rows of value against criteria, with no sense of which ones actually mattered.
02Approach

Verdict first, evidence beside it.

Four principles turned a reference page into a reading order.

Principle 01 Summary on top One score out of ten, its colour band and four pillars — before any table.
Principle 02 Show the weights Every pillar and sub-score carries its percentage, so the arithmetic is visible.
Principle 03 Score next to the data A small score chip sits beside each section and field that feeds the model.
Principle 04 Compare, don’t toggle Applicants become columns in one table instead of tabs to flip between.
Key design decisions
01A section bar under the summaryEducation to Policy — one chip per section, so a nine-section page is never more than one click deep.
02Choose which sources countIncome and EMIs from salary slip, bank, ITR or bureau each get a checkbox, with totals that update as the manager decides.
03Repayment options from the engineRun Decision Engine returns product cards with rate, period and FOIR — each editable from the same data.
04Version the model on screenThe summary shows which scoring model produced it, so reviewers know which rules applied to a case.

Faster reviews, clearer recommendations.

05Learnings

People trust a score they can take apart.

01Show the arithmeticWeights beside every score turned the model from a black box into something a manager could argue with.
02Columns beat tabs for comparisonThe question is always “how do these people compare?” — one table answers it without memory.
03Let judgement override, visiblySource checkboxes and manual deviations keep the manager in charge — and leave a trail of why.
